About

From forest genomes to blood cancer, one sequence at a time.

I'm a doctoral researcher and bioinformatician at Universitätsmedizin / Universität Duisburg-Essen, working within the Department of Hematology and Stem Cell Transplantation at the West German Cancer Center (WTZ). My current research analyses the progression of myelofibrosis, a rare bone marrow cancer, using single-cell sequencing to understand how disease unfolds one cell at a time.

My path here started in forest genomics: as a Junior Project Fellow at the Forest Research Institute (ICFRE), Dehradun, I worked on de novo genome assembly and molecular marker development for non-model plant species — training that shaped how I think about extracting signal from messy, low-coverage sequencing data.

I hold a Master's degree in Bioinformatics from Jamia Millia Islamia, New Delhi, and my broader interests span single-cell RNA-seq, structural bioinformatics, computer-aided drug discovery, and phylogenomics.

Research focus

Five threads, one question: how does a cell decide what it becomes?

01

Single-cell RNA-seq

Resolving cell-state heterogeneity in myelofibrosis progression and other hematological disease models.

02

NGS sequence analysis

Quality control, alignment, and de novo assembly across both clinical and non-model genomic datasets.

03

Structural bioinformatics

Linking sequence and structure to understand protein function and disease-relevant mutations.

04

Computer-aided drug discovery

Applying in-silico methods — docking and structure-based screening — to accelerate target identification.

05

Phylogenomics

Reconstructing evolutionary relationships from genomic and marker data, rooted in my forestry genomics work.
